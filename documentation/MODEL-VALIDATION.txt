Synthetic tests used the real JNI adapter on a Linux x86_64 development host,
not the itel A25 Pro. The native model was given the app's source-constrained
and /no_think prompts. These results do not establish phone speed or medical
accuracy. Concurrent Android compilation also affected timings.

RESULTS
English RAG returned both supplied literal values: cedar-47 and 250.
Urdu RAG preserved digits but changed the literal code cedar-47 to a mixed-script
corruption. Punjabi Gurmukhi returned a question without the requested values.
Punjabi Shahmukhi returned only 250. All three short translation cases failed
script, content or negation checks, often returning only 250 mg. A retrieval
rewrite of an Urdu paracetamol question dropped the named drug and returned
generic medicine keywords.

CONCLUSION
This small candidate is an English RAG experiment, not a proven Urdu/Punjabi
translator. It must not be presented as a reliable medical translation option.
The app can import a different compatible GGUF, but its actual multilingual
quality and fit on a 2 GB phone must be tested separately. A larger model may
need more available RAM than the itel A25 Pro can provide.

The app rejects translations with failed checks and retains the intact saved
original. It protects numeric quantities/units, explicit negation and literal
alphanumeric identifiers, and does not let a failed rewrite replace a better
original keyword match. Such checks are conservative and incomplete; passing
them does not clinically validate a translation or prove semantic equivalence.

Medicine documents remain unverified reference material. This release does not
provide validated diagnosis, automatic prescribing, or a universal emergency
triage system. The bundled Whisper model is separate: it passed a real English
transcription test, but Urdu/Punjabi recognition and all phone audio paths still
need representative device testing.
